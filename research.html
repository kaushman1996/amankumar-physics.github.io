Research

I study how strong electronic interactions and quantum fluctuations give rise to emergent order, unconventional excitations, and transport in quantum materials.

01 · Perspective

Overview & methods

My work centers on interacting quantum many-body systems, particularly where charge ordering, geometric frustration, and fractionalization challenge simple single-particle descriptions. I aim to connect microscopic lattice models with observables such as electrical conductivity, thermal response, and finite-temperature phase transitions.

I use exact diagonalization (including targeted eigensolvers), finite-temperature Lanczos methods, Monte Carlo simulations, and tensor-network approaches, supported by analytical arguments and symmetry considerations.

02 · Electronic crystallinity

Generalized Wigner crystals and quantum melting

In triangular moiré systems, repulsive interactions can stabilize commensurate charge patterns at fractional filling. My research examines how long-range Coulomb interactions, screening, and quantum hopping determine the stability of these generalized Wigner crystals.

I also investigate their finite-temperature melting. The effect of quantum fluctuations is not always a simple suppression of order: for certain first-order transitions, changes in the energy and entropy of competing phases can produce an increase in the melting temperature.

03 · Correlated metals · Ongoing

Pinball liquids and triangular-lattice Hubbard models

A pinball liquid is a partially charge-ordered metal: charge-rich sites form a relatively localized background, while other carriers remain itinerant. It offers a setting to investigate the coexistence of strong electronic crystallinity and metallic conduction.

My current work explores the spinful extended Hubbard model on a triangular lattice at average electron density n = 1/2. Questions of interest include the evolution between metallic, pinball-liquid, and charge-ordered insulating regimes, and the temperature and frequency dependence of electrical transport.

These transport questions are ongoing investigations; this section does not imply a universal resistivity law or an established phase boundary.

04 · Frustrated quantum magnets

Kitaev physics and thermal Hall transport

Kitaev-type spin models provide a route to unconventional excitations and topological phenomena. I study how magnetic fields and additional interactions affect fractionalization, magnetic order, and thermal transport.

My work on Kitaev–Heisenberg systems examines thermal Hall conductivity near field-suppressed magnetic order. A central question is how thermal Hall signatures evolve when the system departs from an idealized spin-liquid limit.

05 · Many-body excitations

Quasiparticle stability and Hilbert-space structure

In strongly interacting systems, excitations can lose their conventional quasiparticle character. I use many-body eigenstates and targeted numerical methods to examine the stability and reconstruction of excitations beyond weak-coupling approximations.

This perspective has informed my work on Kitaev-related spin systems and the random t–J model, connecting spectral structure to the fate of quasiparticle-like excitations.

06 · Research outlook

Current directions

My present interests include correlated transport and metal–insulator transitions in triangular-lattice models, quantum melting in moiré materials, and optical or electromagnetic responses of interacting electrons. I am also interested in strain and orbital-current response in layered graphene systems.

Across these directions, the objective is to formulate experimentally testable diagnostics while keeping the distinction between established results and new hypotheses clear.
