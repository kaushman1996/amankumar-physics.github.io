Overview

From microscopic models to measurable phenomena

My research investigates emergent phenomena in strongly correlated quantum many-body systems, with particular emphasis on electronic crystallinity, fractionalization, topological order, and transport. I study how long-range interactions, geometric frustration, and quantum fluctuations shape phase diagrams and response functions.

My approach combines exact diagonalization, finite-temperature Lanczos methods, Monte Carlo simulations, and tensor-network techniques, informed by symmetry and many-body theory. The goal is to connect microscopic Hamiltonians to experimentally accessible signatures.

01 / Charge order and moiré matter

Generalized Wigner crystals and quantum melting

Long-range Coulomb interactions can stabilize charge-ordered phases at fractional fillings of triangular moiré lattices. I study their stability and finite-temperature melting in extended Hubbard models, including the role of interaction range and electrostatic screening.

An important theme is the competition between classical charge order and quantum motion. In certain regimes, quantum fluctuations modify the free-energy balance between phases and can even raise a first-order melting temperature while reducing the ordered moment.

02 / Correlated metallicity

Pinball liquids and metal–insulator transitions

I investigate the interplay of charge order and itinerancy in triangular-lattice extended Hubbard models, including the spinful system at electron density n = 1/2. A pinball liquid combines a comparatively localized charge-rich sublattice with mobile carriers on the remaining sites.

Current questions include how such partially melted charge order influences metallic transport, optical conductivity, temperature-dependent resistivity, and transitions to fully charge-ordered insulating states. These are active research directions, rather than established universal properties of the phase.

03 / Quantum magnets

Thermal Hall transport and fractionalization

In Kitaev and related frustrated magnets, the thermal Hall response can probe unconventional excitations and the influence of magnetic order. My work uses microscopic approaches to study thermal transport, including tensor-network and finite-temperature methods.

I am interested in how large or nearly quantized responses evolve with field, additional interactions, and competing ordered phases, and in how transport calculations can be compared with candidate Kitaev materials.

04 / Excitations

Quasiparticle stability in many-body Hilbert space

Strong correlations challenge the conventional quasiparticle picture. I have studied quasiparticle stability through the structure of many-body eigenstates, using targeted exact diagonalization and the perspective of localization in Hilbert space.

This framework connects the fate of fractionalized excitations in frustrated magnets with quasiparticle reconstruction in interacting electronic models.

Looking ahead

Current and emerging directions

My ongoing interests include transport in triangular-lattice Hubbard systems, quantum melting and correlated metallicity in moiré materials, finite-temperature tensor networks, and electromagnetic response in layered quantum materials. I am also exploring connections between orbital currents, strain, and collective electromagnetic instabilities in graphene-based systems.

Across these problems, I aim to identify concrete theoretical predictions and observables that can distinguish competing many-body phases.
